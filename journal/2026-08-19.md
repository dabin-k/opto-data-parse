clusterless spike smoothing. Details - Fig S2.A on p15 of the paper pdf + written description on p25: S1.6 Spike Detection. 

Aim of clusterless spike smoothing : derive smoothed spikes which can then be differentiated into wide and narrow spikes. 
Why is this necessary? A single spike is too noisy to be differentiated by their width. 

General idea : denoise the spikes by averaging over spikes in similar regions of feature space

Method 
1. For each target spike, use LSH (locality-sensitive hashing) to find up to 100 spikes of similar waveform on all recording sites 'channels' of the same shank 
2. Average the waveforms of these 100 spikes to obtain a smoothed waveform of the target spike 
3. Use the smoothed spikes to separate wide (putative excitatory) and narrow (putative fast-spiking inhibitory) spikes. Criteria 
- full-width at 2/3 maximum (FW3M)
- trough-to-peak duration 
- early and late gradients of the upstroke (between 0.07 and 0.5ms from the trough time)
4. Filtering 
- spike amplitude, cross-channel waveform variability used for quality control. If amplitude is low or the waveform is similar across channels - exclude 

More on LSH : 
- LSH is assessed by Euclidean distance on the PCA features produced by SpikeDetekt

Notes
- We care about population rates of excitatory and inhibitory populations, and never really care about individual cells. We usually have to go through some spike sorting, but attribution of spikes to particular cells is actually less important for us than E-or-I classification. Be mindful of this - this might reduce how much work we actually have to do.

## FW3M + the "not clusterless" finding (later same day)

- Switched width feature to **FW3M** (full width at 2/3 trough depth, interpolated) —
  window-safe (measured on the trough, not the off-window peak). Ported to
  `figures.py::plot_fw3m_features` (+ `_fw3m_ms`, `_trough_to_peak_ms`,
  `_dominant_trace`, `_load_smoothed_cg`). Matches S2.C scale (median ~0.16–0.19 ms).
- **But our S2.C is unimodal, not the paper's two clusters.** Ran down why:
  - QC criteria (fitting error>0.45, 5th-NN norm dist>0.305) reject ~0% of our spikes
    → our smoothing is clean, not the cause.
  - Averaging `waveforms_raw` (unfiltered, as the paper says) vs `waveforms_filtered`
    gives **identical** FW3M → not the cause.
  - **Root cause: we are NOT clusterless.** `min_cluster_group>=1` keeps only 21%
    (190k) of cg0; the discarded group-0 "noise" clusters are **714k REAL spikes**
    (amp median 332 ADC, all >150 — not noise) and are **systematically narrower**
    (unsmoothed FW3M median 0.101 vs 0.129 kept). Fast-spiking PV (I) cells fire fast,
    isolate poorly, and get dumped into MUA/"noise" — so cluster-filtering discards
    much of the I population, leaving one wide (E) mode. The paper is clusterless
    exactly to avoid this, using waveform QC (not cluster labels) to reject noise.
- Spike-duration "wriggle" in S2.C top = sample quantization (integer argmin/argmax →
  0.0333 ms = 1/30 kHz steps), not an alignment issue. FW3M is smooth (interpolated).

## DECISIVE: 20-sample window is too short for the E-defining features

Corrected an earlier bad assessment (S2.C is NOT log-scale — that was my own hexbin
`bins="log"` — and it uses exactly 2 features per panel, as the caption says).
The real blocker: both S2.C panels need the waveform ~0.5 ms AFTER the trough, but
our 20-sample kwx snippet (trough at sample ~10) ends ~0.3 ms after it.
- I duration ~0.2 ms (trough+6) → in-window 99% ✓
- **E duration ~0.5–0.66 ms (trough+15–20) → in-window 0–1% ✗**
- late/"peak" gradient at 0.5 ms (trough+15) → in-window 1% ✗; early gradient (+2) ✓
So E's defining features are physically off the snippet edge → E shows as a
compressed shoulder, never a blob. Not fixable by any measurement trick.

**Fix requires wider snippets, re-extracted from raw voltage.** The concatenated
`M150605_ICTP1_s1_not9.dat` (14.3 GB) that SpikeDetekt read is present, and kwik
`time_samples` index directly into it → re-extract ±~30-sample windows per spike for
the shank's 8 channels, average over the ALREADY-CACHED neighbours (nbr_idx from PCA
features — unchanged), measure the full features. (Raw reprocessing — propose first.)

## Classification = two conservative 4-D boxes, ~40% kept (S1.6 p26)

The paper classifies only **~40%** of all SpikeDetekt spikes; we currently keep **99%**
(QC passes 99%, then we label every survivor via one trough-to-peak threshold).
The paper instead defines an **E-box** and an **I-box** in 4-D (spike duration, FW3M,
early gradient @0.07ms, late gradient @0.50ms post-trough) and **discards spikes in
neither box** — no bimodal valley needed; that's why S2.C draws boxes. Boundary table:
- duration: I 0.13–0.33, E **0.47–0.83** (dead zone 0.33–0.47 discarded)
- FW3M: I 0.08–0.18, E 0.18–0.30 (session-tuned; adjacent, little discard)
- early grad: I >1.16, E <1.16 µV/ms ; late grad: I <0, E >0
This resolves the earlier "not bimodal" worry: the ~60% discard is driven by the
duration dead-zone + gradient signs — the features that need the wide window. Confirms
re-extraction is mandatory: E-duration (to 0.83ms) and late gradient (@0.50ms) are
off-window in the 20-sample snippet. FW3M+early gradient are window-safe.
